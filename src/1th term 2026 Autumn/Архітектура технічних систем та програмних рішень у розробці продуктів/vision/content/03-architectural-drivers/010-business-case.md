## Business Case

The section lays out the business case for the eDiscovery and Litigation Document Review Platform.

![Business level view of the eDiscovery and Litigation Document Review Platform](business-view.pdf){#fig:business-view width=85%}

@fig:business-view shows the users of the platform, its external systems, and the data flows between them.

For its matters, the Client must preserve, collect, review and produce electronically stored information. Today, three external vendors do this work with different workflows, and contract reviewers read every document. This costs EUR 18.5 million each year. For 68 percent of matters, the review cost is more than the budget. Partners cannot compare cost and progress across matters.

The platform puts the full eDiscovery lifecycle into one firm-operated system:

- The matter-intake and conflicts system sends each new matter and its ethical walls to the platform.
- eDiscovery analysts send legal holds to custodians. Custodians acknowledge the holds on the platform.
- The platform preserves and collects custodian data from corporate and cloud sources, such as Microsoft 365 and Purview.
- The platform processes the data and removes duplicates. Continuous active learning shows the most relevant documents first to review managers and contract reviewers.
- Privilege analytics find privileged content for redaction. Then the platform produces the documents to opposing parties and courts.
- Partners monitor the cost and progress of each matter in a browser or on a tablet. Cost data goes to the practice-management and billing system.
- The platform exchanges documents with the iManage Work document management system. Security administrators manage access and ethical walls.
- In Phase 3, client counsel monitor their matters in a client reporting portal.

Each matter stays in the region that its client approves: the EU, the UK or the US. Per-client keys encrypt the data, and an audit trail records all actions. Thus, the firm can show clients and courts a secure, defensible and auditable review process. The firm can also offer fixed fees, because it controls its review costs.
